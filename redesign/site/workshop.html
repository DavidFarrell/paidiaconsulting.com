What a factory is, in one example

"Factory" means a line of small AI jobs, each passing its work to the next, with a person checking before anything leaves. You build one for your own task.

  1. Step one reads each incoming supplier email and pulls out what is being asked.
  2. Step two drafts the reply and logs it in your tracker.
  3. Step three checks the draft against your rules: tone, price list, what you never promise.
  4. You read it and press send. Twenty seconds instead of ten minutes, forty times a week.

Other tasks people bring: grant report first drafts, weekly board packs from three spreadsheets, course feedback summaries, supplier onboarding checks, the Monday pipeline update. If you do it every week and it starts with reading something, it is a candidate.

4

What you will learn

Less time asking AI questions one at a time. More time running a small line of AI jobs on your own work, and knowing when to trust it.

Say it once, properly
  • Write what you want as instructions the AI follows every time, instead of a prompt you retype. Most of the work is deciding what good looks like.
Cut the task into pieces
  • Small steps the AI does well and you can check. You will learn to spot the step that still looks fine to you but is too big for it.
Let a second step mark the first
  • The step that wrote the draft should not be the one that checks it. Check against your own rules: the price list, the tone, the things you never promise.
Keep a person at the gate
  • Decide what someone reads before anything leaves, where the chain works on its own, and where it stops and asks you.
Fix the recipe, not the output
  • Run it on real examples, note what went wrong, change the instructions. Take steps out when results stop improving; a longer chain is not a better one.

How it runs

Two 90-minute live sessions, the first on Friday 20 November, with a few days between them so you try it on real work before you finish it. Most of the time is spent building, with a teacher looking at your screen.

  1. Session 1Map it and build the first step

    We show a working chain built live. Then you map your own task on one page and get the first AI step running on your real example.

  2. In betweenTry it on real work

    Run step one on three real examples and note what it gets wrong. That list is your material for session 2.

  3. Session 2Chain it and add the check

    Add the second and third steps, decide where a person checks the output, and run the whole chain end to end.

  4. AfterA 45-minute drop-in session

    A week or two later. Bring what broke when it met your real week, and we fix it together.

No. The workshop is for people who do not write code. If you can write a clear email, you can build a chain of AI steps.

Something you do every week that starts with reading: emails, forms, reports, spreadsheets. Tell us in a line when you book and we will say whether it fits.

Which AI tools will we use?+

Whichever you already have: ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot or Gemini. If your organisation only allows one tool, we build with that. A paid plan makes the sessions easier.

Is it recorded?+

We record the demonstrations and teaching and send them within a day. Your own build time and the breakout rooms are never recorded.

What if I miss a session?+

Watch the recording and we will catch you up at the start of the next session, or move you to the next cohort at no cost.

It helps us help you, because we can see when someone is stuck. Hide your self-view if you like.

What if I do not get something working?+

Then we refund you in full. In practice David moves between the breakout rooms so nobody is left stuck.

What if my task is confidential?+

You build on your own AI account, inside whatever your organisation already allows. Bring a version of the task with anything sensitive taken out; the build time is never recorded.

Can my employer pay?+

Yes. We invoice from Paidia Consulting Ltd and accept purchase orders, so your organisation can pay and reclaim the VAT.

Yes. A private version runs on your team's own tasks and tools, for up to 12 people, online or at your office. Get in touch.
