### Notes

> Embodiment is profoundly affected by action. That is Kontra and colleagues: action experience is a powerful tool for learning, right through development and into adulthood. That is what is happening here. Your body does the experiment. You eat the muffin, you wait, you have the poo, and you time it yourself. Then you play, and you feel your own cognitive function fight itself, in games built on the same tests psychologists use. Playing generates real cognitive function data: the Stroop test, and three more games built in the same spirit. Nobody is told any of this. It happens to them.

- This slide carries the arc's thesis: “Blue Poo is embodied learning: you eat the muffin, you have the poo, and then you feel your own cognitive function fight itself in a game built on the same tests psychologists use.”
- Mary's wording throughout is “cognitive function”, not the arc's “executive function”. Her slides and these notes follow her.
- The three places the teaching happens used to sit here. They are now the next slide, which is where the Ma et al reference and the scientists' hypothesis also live.

### Notes

> Here is the premise, and it rests on two things the science already indicates. Your gut is linked to your brain. And brain function can be measured; the Stroop test has been doing it since 1935. The whole journey is six steps: bake, eat, wait, scan, play, and chat. David will take you from the poo to the conversation.
